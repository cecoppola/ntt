## Answer in five lines

1. The two phenomena are the same kind: a **stall in the single-node head of the reciprocal** (`recip_mn_body`: shift, gather to host, `db_from_bi`
   upload, chain) on one node, billed to the other nodes as waiting. At 4 nodes rank 0 bills it into the printed "chain" interval, at 10 nodes
   it lands in the part of the reciprocal that is neither products nor chain.
2. 4-node: spike = +1 s to +86 s added 1:1 to the total; the rest of the reciprocal (19.6 s) and bs are unchanged. It is **episodic and
   persistent** (P(spike | previous run spiked) 24/34 = 71 % against 8/62 = 13 %), **concentrated at the start of every hold** (first 2 runs: 6 of 6;
   rest 29 of 93, Fisher p = 0.001), and **independent** of the slow communication mode (12/29 slow vs 23/70 not, p = 0.49), the arm, and the A13 NIC
   group. The cold-start init of 35-53 s occurs in the same first runs.
3. 10-node blow-ups: bimodal, not a tail. In S31, 26 of 30 runs have "reciprocal minus products minus chain" = 23.5-29.4 s (median 24.4 s, of which
   about 21 s is the known 20-25 s stall of host aac7-n01), and **4 of 30 have 101-175 s** (+77..+151 s). Rate 4/30 = 1 in 7.5, which is the quoted
   "1 in 8-16". Products, bs, chain and everything after the reciprocal are normal in those runs.
4. Best hypothesis (evidence **low-moderate**, mechanism unknown): a variable-length wait inside `db_from_bi` (the 0.9 MB upload that takes 1.0 s in
   every clean run) or in the gather before it, caused by a node/host state that is set per hold and persists for minutes to hours. Not memory
   pressure at the level the logs show (MemAvailable/Cached identical in spike and clean runs).
5. Next: the 30-min 4-node item below prints, per node, which of shift / gather / db_init / first pool get / upload takes the time
   (`NEWTON_MN_RECIP_TS=2`, existing switch) next to a 1 Hz host reclaim sampler. No code change needed for that step.

## What the "chain" interval really is (from the code and the S30/S32 logs)

`recip(mn): the single-node chain ... (N s)` is `t1 - t0` in `recip_mn_body` (`newton_db.c`): shift + `mdb_to_host_all` (the gather of the top of Q to host,
a collective) + `db_init` + `db_from_bi` (upload of 2*kp+2 = 109 k limbs, 0.9 MB) + `recip_db2` (the chain proper). In a clean run each node needs (S30/S32
`NEWTON_MN_RECIP_TS`, 10 nodes): shift 0.02 s, gather 1.23-1.49 s, chain+upload 1.0-1.3 s, i.e. **the 2.3 s is about 95 % gather and upload; the
chain computation is a few tenths of a second**. In S32 `RECIP_TS=2`: db_init 0.000, first pool get 0.000, `db_from_bi` 0.97-1.0 s on every node but one,
20-25 s on host aac7-n01. A 0.9 MB upload taking 1 s is not bandwidth: it is `hipStreamSynchronize(0)` ("nothing of ours still runs on the target"),
the bounce-buffer copy and a second sync, so the 1 s is mostly a wait for earlier GPU work or a first-use cost (inferred from the code, not measured).
Rank 0 prints the interval, so a late node shows in rank 0's chain only if it is late **before or inside the gather**; a node that stalls after the gather
is billed at the first exchange of the sharded steps (the "rest").

## 10-node blow-ups are the same stage, not a scaled mode shift (S31, 30 runs; `archive/results-raw/c10/C10_10node.tsv`)

`rest` = reciprocal - reciprocal products - chain (all printed by the division summary line). Blow-up runs vs the others:

- In the four runs the whole excess (+77 to +151 s over the median rest of 24.4 s) is in the reciprocal; bs, products, and divisions' steps 1, 2 and X_lo Q are inside
  the normal ranges of their arm. This is the same segment as the 4-node spike (head of the reciprocal, before the first sharded exchange).
- The a2a rate of the blow-up runs is the lowest of its arm (A: 4.5, 5.5, 5.6 vs 6.9-7.8; B: 4.7 vs 5.6-6.5); that is an **artefact of the stall** (the first exchanges after
  the stalled node wait for it, which the rate counts), not a slow fabric: bs, which uses the same exchanges 100 s earlier, is normal. This is why dm x rate looks constant
  across the S19c/S30 runs and does not mean "the same slow mode".
- Frequency 4/30 = 13 % (A 3/15, B 1/15; arms not different, p = 0.6). Hold-start effect present (the first run) but 3 more are sporadic later.
- The permanent 20-25 s of rest (26 runs) is the host aac7-n01 stall (S30/S32), seen as the unrelated constant in the table; excluding that host (as the 4-node holds do) leaves rest about 3.3 s.
- Not testable from these logs: which node stalls in a blow-up (S31 ran without `NEWTON_MN_RECIP_TS`), and whether the 4-node and 10-node episodes share the
  hold-start dependence (10-node: 1 of 4 blow-ups is the first run).

## Hypotheses, ranked

| rank | hypothesis | evidence level | for | against / missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a wait inside the recip head on one node: `hipStreamSynchronize(0)` / bounce copy / VMM background-mapper interaction in `db_from_bi`, or the preceding gather; depends on a per-hold node state | low-moderate | the code path is the one S32 proved to stall 20-25 s on one host; 0.9 MB upload costs 1 s in every clean run (a latent wait); stall is additive and confined to the head; persistent episodes; host-independent across two host sets; same vmm area as the 10-node-only segfaults | no per-substage timing in any 4-node log; the S32 stall host is excluded, so the 4-node case is inferred by analogy |
| 2 | a late node entering the gather (a straggler from the end of bs) billed to rank 0's chain | low | collective is inside the interval | bs and products unchanged; T09 wait-stats show barrier 0; the 10-node blow-ups are after the gather |
| 3 | host memory reclaim (Cached 84-160 GB) during first-touch / pinned copy | low | cold hold start has the largest cache (160 GB in T09); S32 host stall node had the largest Cached (113.7 GB) | MemAvailable and Cached equal in spike vs clean runs; hold 12496 spikes at the floor |
