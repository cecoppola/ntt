Reproducing “e to 40 Billion Digits on a Single MI300A Node”

Campaign report · aac6, 4 × AMD Instinct MI300A (ppac-pl1-s24-16), ROCm 7.2.4 · 12–18 September 2026 · full record in ~/ntt/RESULTS.md §17–48 and PLAN.md

The paper describes a pipeline — binary splitting of Σ 1/k!, a Newton division, and a top-down radix conversion, all on a four-prime FP64 NTT with one prime per APU — and reports 40 billion digits in 285.7 s on 256 GB. This campaign built that pipeline from the paper's description, verified it, matched its time and memory, then tested the design choices the paper left open.

Method

Six phases, each gated by measurement (PLAN.md §5–11). Phase 1 tested every hardware claim the paper's numbers rest on with nine microbenchmarks; Phase 1b took the thirteen discrepancies that surfaced and decided each as the paper's, ours, or a configuration difference; Phase 3 built the pipeline as nine modules, each with a GMP-checked test before the next module used it; Phase 4 ran the acceptance ladder; Phase 5 ran the alternatives.

The reference for correctness was GMP itself: e to 10⁹ digits generated once (442 s, SHA-256 per 10⁶-digit block), so every run to 10⁹ is compared byte for byte. Above that, the paper's own tier-1 identity T·(P+Q) ≡ X·Q + R modulo eight 62-bit primes, plus the digit string reduced modulo the same primes against X, plus 50-digit windows.

What the paper does not say, and what was wrong in it

  1. The FP64 modmul is exact only with one lazy operand. Two corrections each way are exact for lazy × canonical; both operands lazy in [0, 2p) fail 0.4–21 % of the time on the two larger primes (exact on P2, P3). The paper's kernels would produce silent wrong answers without a fold before every multiply. Butterfly adds must be integer: u + v with u, v < 2p does not fit a double.
  2. Its modmul rate (775 Gmodmul/s/APU) is not reproducible under any variant — ILP, occupancy, rounding; we measure 1 330–1 370. Its implied 2.1 GHz is a datasheet number: under 64-bit VALU load the shader clock is 1.47–1.60 GHz, a per-APU power limit (550 W cap).
  3. It omits the top-level split. With 64-bit limbs, 10^d·P is a 4.16 × 10⁹-limb product — a 2³²-point transform — but its pools are 2³¹. Only a Karatsuba over halves at 2³¹ pools is consistent with its 128 GB and 12.6 s. Built that way; the unsplit 2³² alternative was measured (2.6× faster for that one product) and rejected: it needs 256 GiB of pools.
  4. Its truncated Newton step is not sound as printed — dropping low limbs of r² perturbs the correction by exactly the precision the step computes. A correction-form iteration with a self-correcting doubling, the high half of A·μ and a low product for X·Q reach its dm time.
  5. NUMA placement is the difference between 3.7 TB/s and 67 GB/s for registered host staging (55×); its “+20 s for interleave” is this mechanism. The first-touch threads must be pinned to the APU's node.

Beyond the paper

5 × 10¹⁰ digits, verified (residues, and windows at 10¹⁰, 2 × 10¹⁰, 3 × 10¹⁰ and 4 × 10¹⁰ from the 40-billion run): 442 s, 375 GB host + 128 GiB pools = 503 GB of the node's 512 GB. That is the ceiling of this pipeline here; 6 × 10¹⁰ does not fit. The ~/ntt goal of 10¹¹ digits on one node needs a memory-resident-plane design, not a denser prime set — the ALGORITHM.md estimate of 3.4 bytes per digit is now corrected to 6.2 bytes per digit plus 192 GB fixed for this structure.

Hardware facts established on the way

  • Tiled FP64 NTT: 1.20 TB/s effective at 2³¹ (paper 1.08); 1.44 TB/s with the register-blocked body. LDS is the limiter: 36 TB/s at 3 blocks/CU, 19 at 1.
  • CPU CRT is compute-bound at 0.4–0.65 s per 2³⁰ whatever the placement; the GPU CRT does 2³¹ in ≈ 0.5 s including the copy back.
  • A kernel storing into registered host memory is 31× the D2H blit (1.8 TB/s vs 58 GB/s); registered memory on the wrong NUMA node reads at 67 GB/s.
  • Launch 3.4 µs, launch + sync 11 µs; streams overlap fully; no TLB cliff on a 64 GiB pinned arena.
  • xGMI: 91 GB/s per direction per pair from one copy kernel, 1.2 µs remote round trip; two of the six pairs share a link bidirectionally.
  • FP64 Barrett modmul 1 340 Gmodmul/s per APU, insensitive to occupancy; FP32 FMA 86 TFLOP/s at the measured 1.5 GHz.

What changed, each on a measurement (RESULTS §50–66): the limb base as a run-time switch (decimal limbs of 10¹⁸ remove 10dP and the radix conversion — 92 s → 11 s); the Newton doubling anchored at the target precision (a binary gain too: 291 → 271 s); the binary-splitting level pools as four device regions with subtree ownership and a locality-aware batch tier (an APU reads its own node at 3.8 TB/s, another's at 93 GB/s; the tier went 44.8 → 27.5 s); transform lengths 3·2ᵏ on a prime set with 3·2⁴⁴ | p−1; the seed span halved; and the top binary-splitting levels and the Newton phase on device-resident numbers through a four-APU distributed transform (reciprocal 29.7 → 12.4 s; the host-side level pools are gone, so decimal's peak host memory fell 233 → 160 GB and the run-to-run spread of its CPU phases, which was page faults, with it: wall 170 ± 12 → 142 ± 1.8 s); and the device products over one 2³¹-point plane split as a cost-minimising grid of pieces instead of halving the longer operand (decimal's 2.2e9²-limb products 8 → 6 planes: dm 48.9 → 37.0 s, phases 119 → 109 s, no memory change; the division's low product skips the pieces above its window). A 45-second linear scan hidden in the wall clock since Phase 3 was found and removed; the paper comparison is restated with both figures.

Multi-node. The distributed four-step transform is verified on the four real APUs over xGMI (one push kernel drives the three links; 3 all-to-alls per product), block-cyclic inside and contiguous at the interfaces — a single-all-to-all inverse to contiguous ownership was shown not to exist. Sized from the measured cell for 2048 nodes at 2 × 400 Gb/s per APU: ≈ 12.5 s of exposed network per 4 × 10¹⁰-per-node with decimal limbs, ≈ 25 s with binary (a distributed radix conversion). A TCP communicator ran the transform across two nodes (8 ranks, every length verified) on this fabric-less cluster. Per-level checkpoint/restart of the binary splitting is implemented and bit-identical on restart up to 10¹⁰.

Recommendation (the decision is the user's): the decimal base — 35 % faster on one node at 31 % less peak host memory (2.2 × the paper design's wall), no distributed radix conversion, digits are the limbs; binary kept as the switch for reproducing the paper.
