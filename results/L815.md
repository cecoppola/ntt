# L8 (Phase 15, PLAN §36.6) — the transform-length family: 15·2ᵏ against 5·2ᵏ / 7·2ᵏ

Branch `p15-L8` from int15 0b80ca3. **Desk work: no pipeline code changed.** Throwaway scripts `tests/l8_*`, outputs and plan
logs in `results/L815/`. aac6 clone `~/ntt-L815` (0b80ca3; built on the login node for `MN_PLAN_ONLY` and the bench).
Times Eastern. Every number is labelled **measured**, **modelled** or **assumed**.

## 0. The answer in one page

**The user's question**: instead of 5·2ᵏ or 7·2ᵏ, is 15·2ᵏ (= 3·5·2ᵏ) worth having?

- **15·2ᵏ is not an alternative to 5·2ᵏ; it is an add-on.** It needs the same prime change (5 | p − 1 for every prime) and a
  radix-5 butterfly inside its radix-15 pass, so whoever builds 15·2ᵏ has built 5·2ᵏ too. On its own ({2ᵏ, 3·2ᵏ, 15·2ᵏ}) it
  adds one length per octave at 1.875 and gains almost nothing on our sizes (modelled: 0 at 10¹¹, −0.04 s averaged over
  4 × 10¹⁰ … 1.4 × 10¹¹ beyond the cost-aware `pick_len`, §2).
- **What 5·2ᵏ buys depends almost entirely on V3's item 2 (`BS_SEED_TERMS` per run, Batch 1 task 1c).** Today, at S = 256,
  the batch tier sits at 1.35× padding for every level from 4 on, and 5·2ᵏ cuts that to 1.12×: **−3.5 s of transform time on
  average over 4 × 10¹⁰ … 1.4 × 10¹¹ (−1.8 … −5.9 s), −5.0 s at 10¹¹** (modelled; V3 assumed −3 s). Tuning S makes the same
  levels fill 2ᵏ or 3·2ᵏ lengths to 1.00–1.03 with no new code; **after tuning, + 5 gains 0.09 s, + 5,15 0.18 s, + 5,7 0.10 s, + 5,7,15
  0.18 s (at most 0.48 s at any size)** (modelled).
- **With Batch 2's fusion (item 3 / 2a)** the non-power-of-two lengths get cheap at small k and the families gain a little more
  after tuning: **+5 −0.30 s, +15 −0.63 s, +5,15 −0.88 s, +5,7 −0.46 s, +5,7,15 −0.90 s** beyond fused-and-tuned today
  (modelled, one-node sizes averaged). This is the only regime where 15·2ᵏ is worth more than 5·2ᵏ, and it is < 0.5 % of the
  10¹¹ wall.
- **At the 576 target**: the grid steps are counts of pieces at the 2⁴⁰ cap, and **no family moves them** (185 / 197 / 229 pieces
  at 4.25 / 4.30 / 4.40 × 10¹³ in every mode, modelled). The mn tier's lengths are powers of two only; giving it 3·2ᵏ is worth
  −0.5 s and +5 / +5,15 / +5,7,15 −0.8 / −0.8 / −1.0 s (modelled; the sharded reciprocal only). The leaf's critical (top) node
  already pads 1.07× at S = 256 and no family changes its lengths.
- **Cost**: three (or four, below) new primes with 15 | p − 1 (they exist and are *better* than today's: three-prime bound
  8.0 × 10¹⁰ terms against 5.8 × 10¹⁰), roots and constants, a radix-5 and a radix-15 pass (forward, inverse, the fused
  pointwise inverse, `pick_len`, the B/C dist forms, the mn tier's general map for the target), re-verification of every
  length: ≥ 2 weeks (V3's estimate stands). A radix-5 or radix-15 pass costs the same as today's radix-3 pass per point
  (memory-bound, §4).
- **Recommendation**: **do not build 5·2ᵏ, 7·2ᵏ or 15·2ᵏ.** Do item 2 (it takes the whole padding gain) and item 3 (the fusion,
  −6…−8 s). Revisit only if (a) the prime set has to change anyway (it does — see the blocker below) *and* (b) after items 2 and 3
  a measured 10¹¹ run still shows > 2 s of padding waste in the batch tier or the mdev level. If the primes are changed for the
  blocker, **choose them with 15 | p − 1** (§3): it costs nothing and keeps 5·2ᵏ / 15·2ᵏ open.

### ⚠ Found on the way: two blockers for the target that do not depend on the length family

The target plan (`MN_PLAN_ONLY=4.25e13:576`, `results/L815/L815_plan576.txt`) forms mn-tier planes of **2³⁴ … 2⁴⁰ points** (tree
levels from 8 nodes up, the sharded reciprocal from 2³⁴, both division products at 2⁴⁰), with pieces of up to
590 277 777 779 + 472 222 222 223 limbs. Neither the roots nor the three primes support that:

1. **Roots beyond 2³³ are wrong (silently).** `modarith.h ec_root(i, logn)` is `ec_W33[i]^(2^(33 − logn))`; for logn > 33 the
   shift is negative (undefined; on x86-64 the count wraps to 64 − k) and the "root" is **1** for logn = 34 … 41 on all three
   primes (**measured**: `tests/l8_root.c`, `results/L815/l8_root.out`). `ntt_dist.c plan_create` (the mn tier's
   `dist_plan_create_shared`) calls `ec_root(prime, logR + logC)`, so every mn transform above 2³³ points — first at tree level 3
   (8 nodes, 2³⁴) — would be garbage. T1 would catch it, after the run. Never exercised: the multi-node runs so far used at
   most 3 nodes (aac6 has three; cap ≤ 2³²). The primes themselves allow 2⁴⁴…2⁴⁸ (c·2⁴⁴ + 1); the fix is a root from the generator,
   `g^((p − 1) >> logn)`, with a check `logn ≤ v₂(p − 1)` (and the same for `ec_root3`).
2. **Three primes cannot hold the target's pieces.** A piece's coefficient is at most nterms·(10¹⁸ − 1)², nterms = min(pa, pb);
   p₀p₁p₂ = 2^155.36 allows 58 424 467 928 terms (2^35.77, `ec_np3_max_terms`). The target's largest piece has 472 222 222 223
   terms (2^38.78) — **8.1× over**; at the 2⁴⁰ cap the need is 2^158.6, and **no three primes below 2⁵² reach it** (at most
   2^156; `tests/l8_primes.py`). `mn_core` calls `ec_np_check(nc)`, so the run would **stop loudly** at tree level 5 (32 nodes:
   28 579 148 066 + 30 209 369 707 = 58 788 517 773 > 58 424 467 928). Options: **four primes in the mn tier** (the current
   four have product 2^206.6 and 2⁴⁴ | p − 1: margin 2⁴⁸ at the cap) — `ECALC_NP=4` everywhere is **291.2 s instead of 244.9 s
   at 4.25 × 10¹³** (+46 s, +19 %; `mn_model` with `np=4`, **modelled**), a per-product count (three below the bound, four above:
   ≈ +36 s, modelled from the same run's tree / reciprocal / division increments); or pieces capped at 2³⁶ terms (≈ 8× the
   pieces at the top levels: much worse). The mn planes grow by a third (45.9 → 61.2 GB per node at the cap, modelled from the
   plan's 3-prime figure), which the 480 GB budget must absorb (434 / 399 GB today, PLAN §36.10).

**Every 576-node number in PLAN §36.10 (4.1 min, 434 GB) assumes three primes at the 2⁴⁰ cap and is therefore not achievable as
modelled.** This is outside L8's brief (no code changed here); it needs the integrator's and the user's decision before any
576-node run, and it is the natural moment to pick a prime set with 15 | p − 1 (§3).

## 1. The transform lengths and their share of the time

**Method.** The logs of recent 10¹¹ runs print the batch tier per level (`max_nl`, `ntt` seconds, **measured**) but not the
lengths; the code chooses them in `rns_mul.c pick_len` (2ᵏ, or 3·2ᵏ⁻² when nc fits it, k − 2 ≥ `NTT_LOGN_MIN` = 10; one length
per level = the level's largest product, nc = 2·max_nl). Everything above the batch tier — the mdev levels, the reciprocal, the
division — comes from the code's own plan printer, `MN_PLAN_ONLY=1e11:1` (login node, 0.04 s), which prints every product with
its length and form (B: one prime per APU, ≤ 3·2²⁹ points = the 12 GiB pool; C: a 2³¹ plane over the four APUs). The plan
reproduces the run's grids exactly (e.g. the division's 7 × 7 pieces, the reciprocal's 9 × 3). Above the batch tier the
transform time is **modelled** (3 transforms × pieces × the per-point cost of §4; C form ¾ of it).

**10¹¹, one node, S = 256** (`tests/l8_dist.py`, `results/L815/l8_dist.out`; batch tier measured, the rest modelled; 63.8 s of
transforms in all):

| 3·2¹⁰ … 3·2²⁸ (levels 4–22, one per level) | 1.1 × 10⁷ | 22.4 (meas.) | 35.1 % | **1.347–1.350 every level** |

## 2. Time-weighted padding and the modelled saving per family

the cap, filled by the grid. So a family either hits that one position or gains nothing there.

## 3. The prime change

**Today** (`modarith.h`, `EC_PRIMES=1`): p = c·2⁴⁴ + 1, c = 240, 216, 207 (and 147 for four primes): p₀ = 15·2⁴⁸ + 1,
p₁ = 27·2⁴⁷ + 1, p₂ = 207·2⁴⁴ + 1, p₃ = 147·2⁴⁴ + 1. Only p₀ has 5 | p − 1 (and so 15); p₃ alone has 7. Three-prime product
2^155.355 → at most **58 424 467 928 terms** (2^35.766).

**The size bound the code needs**: 2⁵¹ < p (μ₁₁₅ = ⌊2¹¹⁵/p⌋ in 64 bits; `ec_canon64` shifts by 51) and 1.51 p + 2⁵¹ < 2⁵³
(`ntt.c mm_raw`, the reduced-correction modmul's exact fma), i.e. **p < 0.99338 · 2⁵²** — c·2⁴⁴ + 1 with c = 255 already fails.
2⁴⁰ | p − 1 for the 576 cap. Search: all p = k·2⁴⁰ + 1 in the bound (`tests/l8_primes.py`, `results/L815/l8_primes.out`,
Miller–Rabin, primitive roots found):

| need | primes in the bound | best three (product, max terms, margin at 2³³) | best four |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 \| p − 1 (today's family) | 58 | 2^155.853, 8.25 × 10¹⁰ | 2^207.760 |
| **15 \| p − 1** (5·2ᵏ, 15·2ᵏ) | 17 | **3975·2⁴⁰ + 1, 3945·2⁴⁰ + 1, 15·2⁴⁸ + 1 (= p₀): 2^155.809, 8.00 × 10¹⁰ terms, 9.3×** | + 1785·2⁴¹ + 1: 2^207.611 |
| 105 \| p − 1 (+ 7·2ᵏ) | **4** | 1785·2⁴¹ + 1, 3465·2⁴⁰ + 1, 2835·2⁴⁰ + 1: 2^155.029, 4.66 × 10¹⁰ terms, 5.4× | all four (+ 2205·2⁴⁰ + 1): 2^206.136 |
| 9 \| p − 1 (9·2ᵏ, for completeness) | 21 | 2^155.387, 5.97 × 10¹⁰ | 2^207.135 |

- **The 15 | p − 1 set is better than today's** (+37 % three-prime terms, margin 9.3× at 2³³ against 6.8×) and keeps p₀. Its two new
  primes have v₂(p − 1) = 40: the 2ᵏ part is limited to 2⁴⁰ — exactly the 576 cap (mn_logn_cap = 31 + ⌊log₂ 576⌋ = 40); a
  1024-node run (2⁴¹) would need 1785·2⁴¹ + 1 or 1665·2⁴¹ + 1 in their place (product 2^155.7).
- **7 needs 105 | p − 1 and only four such primes exist in the bound**; the best three lose 20 % of the bound (4.66 × 10¹⁰ terms,
  margin 5.4× at 2³³ — still inside every one-node product, nterms ≤ 2³⁰) and a four-prime set has no choice left.
- **At the 2⁴⁰ cap no three-prime set works** (need 2^158.6, see §0's blocker); every four-prime set above does (margin ≥ 2⁴⁷).

**What changes with the primes** (all behind a build switch like `EC_PRIMES`, digits unchanged by construction):
`modarith.h` ec_P, ec_G, ec_MU115, the 2ᵏ root table (`ec_W33` → a root of order 2^v₂ with `EC_LOGN_MAX` = 40, which also fixes
blocker 1), `ec_W3X33` (order 3·2ᵏ), new order-5·2ᵏ / 15·2ᵏ roots, `ec_has_radix3` → per-radix predicates; `crt.c` Garner
constants and `ec_np3_max_terms` (computed at init from ec_P: no edit), `rns_mul.c` `struct gconst` (computed), the `rns_init`
line that prints "c = 240, 216, 207", `ec_words_to_dec3`'s bound comment (< 2^156 still holds); `tests/t_params` /
`results/0_params.txt`, `tests/t_primes` (the margins), `bench/15_barrett_f64.c`, `bench/20_cpu.c` (hard-coded values);
`mem_model.py` / `mn_model.py` (no prime values; the three-prime bound if a model checks it). **Unchanged**: the T1/T2
verification primes `verify.c t1_q` (62-bit, distinct from any 52-bit NTT prime), the digit references (`e_1e11.out`, sha1),
every checkpoint format that stores digits or T1 residues. **Invalidated**: any saved state holding NTT residues across a
restart (none known — the checkpoints hold limbs).

## 4. The cost of a radix-5 / radix-15 pass

**Measured basis (V3, `results/V314/bench.log`)**: today's radix-3 pass (`ntt3.c k_r3_fwd`, a separate pass before the 2ᵏ
transform) costs **16.3 ms per 3·2²⁹ points = 10.1 ps per point at every k** (1.58 TB/s at 16 B per point: memory-bound), which is
19.8 % of a 3·2²⁹ transform and 50 % at 3·2¹⁰ (small 2ᵏ transforms are cheap per point: 10.5 ps at 2¹⁰ against 42 ps at 2²⁹).
Its arithmetic is naive: 8 canonical modmuls per 3 points (2.7 per point).

**Estimate (modelled)**: a pass reads and writes each point once whatever the radix, so while the arithmetic stays under the
memory time the cost per point is the same. Naive DFTs in the same style: radix 5 = 24 modmuls per 5 points (4.8 per point,
1.8× radix-3), radix 7 = 48 per 7 (6.9), radix 15 as 3 × 5 Cooley–Tukey = 104 per 15 (6.9), radix 15 naive = 224 per 15 (14.9).
At ≈ 12 FP64 instructions per modmul against ≈ 30 × 10¹² FP64 lane-instructions/s per APU (61 TFLOP/s vector FP64), the radix-3
pass uses ≈ 10 % of the compute at 99 G points/s; radix 5 ≈ 19 %, radix 7 and 15-CT ≈ 27 %, naive 15 ≈ 58 %: **still
memory-bound, ≈ 10 ps per point each** (naive 15 borderline), plus 5–15 % for the larger twiddle reads. So a 5·2ᵏ or 15·2ᵏ transform costs per point what a 3·2ᵏ costs today; a 15·2ᵏ built as two passes (r3, then
r5) costs two. Fused into the first 2ᵏ pass (Batch 2 item 3): radix 3 recovers ≈ 70 % (V3, assumed); radix 5 needs 5 tiles per
work-group and radix 15 fifteen — LDS and register pressure, so ≈ 50–70 % (assumed).

**Node microbenchmark**: `tests/l8_rbench.c` (stand-alone; radix 3 / 5 / 7 / 15-CT / 15-naive passes in `k_r3_fwd`'s style plus
a copy kernel for the memory ceiling; each checked against the direct formula). Results: see §4a (pending the node window).

## 5. Recommendation and conditions

| option | one node (10¹¹) | 576 target | cost | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **item 2** (S per run; no new lengths) | −9.6 s of modelled transforms (sizes averaged); −6.6 s measured at 10¹¹ (V3, one run) | −3 s (leaf, modelled) | ½ day + a series | **do it** (Batch 1 1c) |
| **item 3** (fused radix-3, cost-aware pick_len) | −6…−8 s (V3) | −3…−4 s | 2–3 d | **do it** (Batch 2 2a) |
| + 5·2ᵏ | −3.5 s at S = 256; **−0.1 s after item 2**; −0.3 s after items 2 + 3 | −0.3 s mn (needs mn-tier radix + new primes) | ≥ 2 weeks, new primes | **no** |
| + 15·2ᵏ alone | ≈ 0 (−0.04 s beyond the cost pick) | −0.5 s (= +3 in the mn tier) | as 5·2ᵏ (needs its radix-5) | **no** |
| + 5·2ᵏ, 15·2ᵏ | −0.18 s after item 2; −0.9 s after 2 + 3 | −0.8 s | as 5·2ᵏ + a radix-15 pass | **no** |
| + 7·2ᵏ | +0.02…0.1 s over +5 | −0.2 s over +5,15 | a 105 \| p − 1 set (only four primes exist; −20 % bound) | **no** |

- **Dependence on Batch 2 item 3 (2a)**: yes. Unfused, a non-2ᵏ pass costs 10 ps per point, which at small k is as much as the
  whole 2ᵏ transform — the cost-aware pick then prefers 2ᵏ⁺² and the families lose most of their value. Fused, the families'
  post-tuning value rises 3–5× (to ≤ 0.9 s at 10¹¹) — still below the bar.
- **The grid steps at the target** are counts of pieces at the 2⁴⁰ cap; **no family moves them** (§2). Only a larger cap (memory)
  or fewer pieces per level would.
- **Condition to revisit**: after items 2 and 3 are merged, a measured 10¹¹ run whose batch-tier levels or mdev one-plane
  products still pad > 1.15 with > 2 s at stake. If the prime set is changed for blocker 2 (four primes at the target), pick
  15 | p − 1 primes (§3) so that the option stays open at no cost.
