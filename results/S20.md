## 3. D3: MN_WAIT_STATS at 10 nodes (m)

Counter definition: barrier counts are 0 everywhere (the compute phases have no barriers). "Wait" = time blocked in `comm_wait` (exchange
completion = transfer + peer skew), **summed over the APU threads** (4 per node, a), per node; "recip" is the reciprocal inside dm and is its own
bucket; the peer skew proper (mailbox `wait_ge`/`wait_ne` in comm_shmem.c) is not timed separately. Totals are node sums; "per thread" divides by 4 (a).

| recip | 95.8 (101.9 / 90.9) | 111.7 (115.2 / 108.9) | 24.0 | | |

| **all** | **802.5** | **832.4** | **200.6 (a)** | total 432.2 / 388.1 | |

- Counts per node: bs about 21-22 k waits, dm about 20 k, recip about 19-24 k, other 68.
- **Skew bound.** The least-waiting node is the last to arrive, so (mean - min) and (max - min) bound the skew part: dm 51-119 s summed (**13-30 s per
  thread**, a), bs 27-61 s (7-15 s per thread). The reciprocal's spread is tiny (91-102 s), so it is nearly pure transfer/serial-chain wait. The
  same ranks are high in both arms (dm: ranks 0-2 highest at 410-460; bs: ranks 8-9), so the imbalance is structural, not random.
- **Against S19B's 215-250 s "other" (non-exchange) block:** the barrier/comm_wait counters do not reproduce an "other" wait block. The ~200 s per thread of
  comm_wait (a) is *larger* than S19B's in-flight exchange time (136-151 s per thread), so most of what S19B called "waiting for peers"
  is the exchange itself; the 50-65 s per-thread difference (a: depends on the thread count) may be wait that S19B's post-to-completion window excluded. The rest
  of the 215-250 s (about 150 s) is therefore local work or waits outside `comm_wait` (post-side mailbox waits are not covered). **Unresolved**, not explained.
- **B has the shorter wall but not the lower wait** (832 vs 802 s in this pair; recip +16 s): this one pair suggests the T2048 gain is local
  (compute/pool) time, not exchange wait. One pair only (n=1).

### S-2 / E1 overlap: go or no-go

- **Size of the exposed wait:** about 76 s (bs) + 122 s (dm+recip) per thread (a) at 10 nodes, 10 times the 30 s threshold, even if only the dm part is
  addressed. An upper bound: overlap hides at most min(local work, wait) per level. dm's local work is about 223 - 122 = 101 s (a), so the ceiling is near
  100 s; a realistic 25-50 % would be 30-60 s at 10 nodes (a, no measurement).
- **Recommendation: GO on a prototype of the division overlap (S-2)**, as the largest identified block (confidence: medium). The prototype should first
  target the reciprocal and division exchanges (transfer-dominated: reciprocal skew is about 0, dm skew only 13-30 s of 122).
- **Ready counter in `wait_ge`/`wait_ne`: not needed to start.** Skew is bounded above by 13-30 s per thread (a) and the node-spread already
  gives the split. Add it only if the prototype's gain falls short of the model (it would then say whether the remainder is skew).
- Caveats: 10 nodes on a Slingshot-class fabric at an effective 6 GB/s per APU thread; the target's fabric is about 8x faster (S19B), so the wait
  there is smaller and the S-2 benefit must be re-derived from `estimate.py` before it is counted on the target.
